Image Compressor

Image Compressor saves your picture as a JPEG at a quality you choose. The Quality slider runs from 10% to 100% and starts at 80%. Pixel dimensions do not change, so the file gets smaller only because the encoder keeps less fine detail. Transparent areas are filled with white first, because JPEG has no transparency.

Open Image Compressor

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

You can also enter an optional maximum size in kilobytes, from 1 to 25,000. In that case the tool tries up to nine qualities between 10% and your slider value, using a binary search, and keeps the highest quality that fits under the limit. The slider therefore acts as a ceiling. If even the lowest quality is too large, the tool tells you to reduce the dimensions first or raise the target.

When to use Image Compressor

  • A form, email or website rejects your photo for being larger than a given size, such as 500 KB.
  • You want faster page loading for a photo that you plan to publish.
  • You need to reduce a batch of camera photos before sharing them.

Step by step

  1. Upload the image. The status line shows its current file size.
  2. Open Image Compressor. Leave the quality at 80% for a general-purpose reduction, or lower it for a smaller file.
  3. If a site requires a size limit, type it in the optional maximum size field, in KB.
  4. Press Compress image and read the download button, which shows the resulting format and size.
  5. If the target cannot be reached, use Resize Image to reduce the dimensions and try again. Download when the size is right.

Tips for better results

  • Reduce the pixel dimensions first when the picture is larger than it needs to be. A 4000-pixel photo shown at 1200 pixels wastes most of its bytes.
  • Qualities between 70% and 85% usually look nearly identical to the original on screen while saving a large share of the size.
  • Do not compress the same file repeatedly. Each JPEG save loses a little more detail, so always work from the original.
  • Photos with large smooth areas, like skies, show banding earlier than detailed photos when quality is very low.

Common mistakes

  • Compressing a transparent PNG logo to JPEG and finding a white background. Keep logos as PNG or WebP.
  • Setting a very low size target for a large photo without resizing. The tool will report that the target is unreachable.
  • Assuming that a lower quality always makes the file smaller. An already optimized JPEG can grow slightly when re-encoded at a higher quality.

What to expect

JPEG is lossy and fills transparency with white. A smaller file is not guaranteed for an already optimized source.

For a 500 KB limit, try a moderate quality; if even the minimum quality is too large, reduce dimensions.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Questions about Image Compressor

How does the size target work?

The tool repeatedly tries qualities between 10% and your slider value and keeps the best one that is at or below the target. It stops after nine attempts, so the result is close to, but not always exactly at, the limit.

Does compression change the dimensions?

No. Width and height stay the same. To change them, use Resize Image.

Why was my transparent image given a white background?

JPEG cannot store transparency. The tool fills transparent areas with white before encoding. Use Convert Image to export PNG or WebP if you need to keep transparency.
