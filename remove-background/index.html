Foreground cutout · Browser-based workflow

Remove Background

Remove Background separates a subject from its surroundings and returns the subject on a transparent canvas. The first time you use it, your browser downloads TensorFlow.js and the DeepLab segmentation model (from the jsDelivr CDN), then the model runs locally on your device. Your photo is not uploaded to a server for this step.

Open Remove Background

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

DeepLab was trained on the PASCAL VOC categories, which cover people, animals and a small set of everyday objects such as bottles, chairs, sofas, cars and potted plants. That is why portraits and pets usually cut out well, while an object outside those categories, such as a watch, a handbag or a jar of cosmetics, may not be recognized. Every recognized category is kept, so a person sitting on a sofa keeps the sofa as part of the foreground. When the model cannot load or finds no subject, the tool switches to an edge-color method that guesses the background from the border pixels and tells you that the fallback was used.

When to use Remove Background

  • You need a transparent PNG of a person or pet for a flyer, slide or profile image.
  • You want a clean cutout before running Background Changer, Blur Background or Product Photo Editor, which all rely on the same subject detection.
  • You are preparing several photos taken against a plain wall or sheet, where even the fallback method performs reasonably well.

Step by step

  1. Upload a JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF or HEIC image. Files up to 25 MB are accepted.
  2. Open Remove Background and press Apply. Wait for the status line to say that the model finished or that the fallback was used.
  3. Zoom the result to 100% and inspect hair, fingers, glasses and any thin straps, which are the places a mask most often fails.
  4. If the edges look wrong, use Undo and try a version of the photo with stronger separation between subject and background, for example a higher-contrast or better-lit original.
  5. Download the result as PNG. JPEG cannot store transparency and would fill the empty area with a solid color.

Tips for better results

  • Contrast between subject and background matters more than image size. A medium-resolution photo with a clean separation beats a large photo where the subject blends into the scene.
  • Check the result on both a dark and a light backdrop. A faint bright rim around the subject is invisible on white and obvious on black.
  • Shoot or choose images where the subject is complete in the frame. Cropped limbs at the border are harder for the model to close properly.
  • Run this tool once. Applying it repeatedly to an already transparent image only hardens the edges that are already there.

Common mistakes

  • Exporting the cutout as JPEG. The transparent area becomes white or black and the benefit of the cutout is lost; use PNG or WebP.
  • Expecting every object type to be recognized. If the status line reports the fallback, treat the result as a rough draft and review the edges closely.
  • Using a busy photo where the background has the same colors as the subject. No segmentation approach can invent a boundary that the image does not show.

What to expect

Busy backgrounds, transparent objects and unknown object classes may need manual cleanup. No model can guarantee perfect edges.

A product against a plain wall is easier to isolate than the same product in a crowded display.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Models and libraries, where a tool needs them, download from public content networks. See the privacy policy for the full list.

Questions about Remove Background

Is my photo uploaded when I remove the background?

No. The model files are downloaded to your browser and the image is processed on your device. Only the model and library files are fetched from public CDNs, which see an ordinary network request.

Why did the result say a fallback was used?

The segmentation model could not be loaded, for example because of a blocked CDN or a very slow connection, or it did not recognize a subject. The edge-color fallback samples the border of the picture and removes similar colors, so it works best on plain backgrounds.

Why are the edges around hair not perfect?

The model produces a black-and-white mask at its own working resolution, which the tool scales up to your photo and softens with a small blur. Fine hair has semi-transparent edges that a mask like this cannot reproduce exactly, so zoom in and judge the result at the size you will actually use.
