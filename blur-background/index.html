Blur Background

Blur Background keeps your subject sharp and blurs everything behind it. The tool finds the subject with the same segmentation model used by Remove Background, blurs a copy of the whole image, and places the sharp cutout on top. You choose a Blur Type and a Blur Amount from 10% to 100% (default 50%).

Open Blur Background

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

Gaussian is a standard smooth blur. Lens is a softer blur with a slightly brighter look, similar to an out-of-focus background. Motion is a directional streak. The blur radius scales with the image size, reaching about 5% of the longest side at 100%, so a 2000-pixel-wide photo at 50% uses a radius of about 50 pixels. Motion blur is an approximation of a streak, not a physical lens simulation.

When to use Blur Background

  • A portrait or pet photo has a distracting background that you want to hide without removing it.
  • You want a depth-of-field look on a photo taken with a phone that has a deep focus range.
  • You are preparing a product or profile image and want attention on the subject.

Step by step

  1. Upload a photo with a clearly visible subject.
  2. Open Blur Background and choose Gaussian, Lens or Motion.
  3. Set the Blur Amount. Begin around 40 to 60% for a natural look.
  4. Press Blur Background and wait for the model. The first run downloads it.
  5. Zoom to 100% and look at the boundary between subject and background for halos or cut-off hair, then download.

Tips for better results

  • A moderate blur looks more natural. Very high amounts make the subject look pasted onto a smear.
  • Check around hair and fingers, where cutout errors are most visible once the background becomes soft.
  • Photos where the subject is a person, animal or recognized object work best; unusual objects may fall back to a rough method.
  • Use Lens for a photographic look and Motion only for deliberate speed effects.

Common mistakes

  • Using the maximum amount, which produces an obvious halo around the subject.
  • Applying the effect to an image where the subject is not recognized. The status line will mention the fallback, and edges may be poor.
  • Expecting realistic depth. The blur is uniform across the background, while a real lens blurs progressively with distance.

What to expect

Cutout quality limits the effect. Directional blur is an approximation, not a physical lens simulation.

A moderate Gaussian blur can separate a portrait from a busy background without an exaggerated halo.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Questions about Blur Background

Why is there a halo around my subject?

The subject cutout is not perfect. Blurred background colors leak around the edges, especially around hair. Lower the amount or start from a photo with a cleaner separation.

Is the blur realistic?

It is a uniform blur behind the subject. A real lens blur grows with distance from the focal plane, so this is an approximation.

Can I blur only part of the background?

Not in this tool. The whole background behind the detected subject is blurred equally.
