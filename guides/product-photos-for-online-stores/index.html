Product Photos for Online Stores: Backgrounds, Consistency and File Prep

Shoppers cannot touch what they buy, so photographs carry the whole job of the product page. You do not need a studio to make good ones. What matters is a clean backdrop, even light, a consistent look across the catalog, and a file that loads quickly. This guide covers the shooting basics and the editing steps.

Shoot for an easy edit

The less you need to fix later, the better the result. Place the product on a plain, light surface, such as white paper or a pale wall, and keep it away from the edges of the frame. A clear border between the product and its background helps every cutout method, including the simple color-based fallback in Remove Background.

Use soft, even light. A large window, with the product facing it and a white card on the opposite side to bounce light into the shadows, is enough for small items. Avoid direct flash, which creates hard reflections and sharp shadows. Keep the camera at a steady height and distance from shot to shot, so that products look the same size and angle.

Show the product from the angles buyers need

  • A clear main image of the whole product, facing the camera.
  • Two or three alternate angles, such as the back, the side and a detail of material or stitching.
  • A scale reference, such as a hand holding the product or a common object next to it, when size is hard to judge.
  • One lifestyle image, if appropriate, to show the product in use.

Photo Collage Maker can combine several angles into a single grid image, for example four views in a 1600 × 1600 two-column layout with 16-pixel gaps, which is useful for a social post or a comparison.

Clean background and cutout

Many marketplaces expect a plain white or very light background. If your product is not photographed on white, use Remove Background to cut it out, then Background Changer to place it on White Studio or Light Gray. Check the edges at 100% zoom against both white and a dark backdrop, to catch any halo.

Be aware that the segmentation model recognizes a limited set of object types. Many products, such as jewelry, cosmetics and tools, are not in that set, and the tool then uses a color-based fallback. That works well on a plain background and poorly on a busy one, which is another reason to shoot on a clean surface.

Consistency across the catalog

Buyers compare items. If every photo has a different crop, margin and shade of white, the shop looks careless. Decide on one canvas shape, such as a square, one margin around the product, and one background color, and apply them to every image. Crop to the same ratio with Crop Image, and keep the product at about the same proportion of the frame.

Shadows and reflections

A product that floats on pure white looks pasted. A soft shadow below it restores weight. Product Photo Editor adds a white canvas, margins, a blurred shadow and a faded reflection in one step. It enlarges the image: a 1000 × 1000 photo becomes 1240 × 1465, so crop and resize afterward if the marketplace wants a specific size.

Check marketplace rules before you add effects. Some platforms require pure white backgrounds with no props, shadows or reflections for the main image. In that case, use the effect only for your own website or social posts.

Size, format and compression

  1. Crop to the shape you need.
  2. Resize to the size the platform recommends, typically at least 1000 to 2000 pixels on the longest side so that zoom works. Use Resize Image with the aspect lock on.
  3. Export as JPG for photographs on a white background, or PNG if you need transparency.
  4. Compress with Image Compressor. A quality of 80% usually keeps a clean look while saving a large share of the size.
  5. Use file names that describe the product, because they help both you and search engines.

A final check

Open the finished image next to two or three others from your catalog. The color of the white should match, the product should be a similar size, and edges should be clean. If one image stands out, redo it before it goes live.

Edit photos to show the product accurately. Adjust brightness and white balance so that the colors match the real item. A picture that looks better than the product leads to returns and complaints.
