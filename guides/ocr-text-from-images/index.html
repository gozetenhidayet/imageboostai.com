Extracting Text From Images With OCR: How to Get Accurate Results

Optical character recognition, or OCR, turns a picture of text into text you can edit and search. It is a useful tool and also an imperfect one. The difference between a clean result and a jumble of wrong characters almost always comes from the image you give it.

How OCR works

An OCR engine first finds blocks of text and splits them into lines and then into individual characters or short sequences. A trained recognizer then decides which character each shape most likely is, helped by a model of the chosen language, which makes some letter combinations more likely than others.

ImageBoostAI uses Tesseract, a widely used open-source engine, through the Tesseract.js library. It runs in your browser. The library and the language data are downloaded the first time you use it, and the image itself is processed on your device.

Prepare the image

Good input matters more than any setting. Work through this checklist before you press Extract Text:

  • Resolution. As a rule of thumb, letters should be at least 20 to 30 pixels tall. For small print on a photographed page, shoot closer or enlarge the image first with Image Upscaler, understanding that enlarging cannot add detail that was not captured.
  • Contrast. Dark text on a plain light background works best. Use Brightness & Contrast to increase contrast on faded or gray scans.
  • Straightness. Hold the camera parallel to the page. Skewed or curved lines reduce accuracy.
  • Light. Avoid shadows across the text and glare from glossy paper.
  • Cropping. Crop to only the text you need, with Crop Image. Pictures, borders and neighboring columns can be read as garbage characters.

Choose the right language

The language setting tells the engine which character set and which patterns to expect. Choosing English for a German page loses the umlauts, and choosing a Latin-script language for an Arabic or Japanese page produces nonsense. The tool offers English, Turkish, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Arabic, Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Korean. Pick the one that matches the main language of the text.

Running the extraction

  1. Upload the image and open Image to Text OCR.
  2. Select the language and press Extract Text.
  3. Wait while the engine and language data load on first use. The status line then shows reading progress.
  4. Read the recognized text in the editable box and correct any errors.
  5. Use Copy text, or download a plain TXT file.

What OCR does poorly

  • Handwriting. The engine is built for printed text. Neat block capitals may work in part, while cursive does not.
  • Decorative or very stylized fonts, and text on busy backgrounds.
  • Tables and multi-column layouts. The text may come out in the wrong order or merged across columns, so extract one column at a time.
  • Low-resolution screenshots and photos with motion blur.

Always proofread

OCR errors are subtle. The most common are confusing 0 and O, 1 and l, rn and m, a missing decimal point, and lost accents. These slip through easily, and they are the characters that matter most in names, numbers and dates. Compare the numbers in the result against the image, especially for invoices, contracts or anything financial or legal.

Recognition runs in your browser, so images are not sent to our servers for processing. If the picture contains sensitive personal information, still be careful where you save and paste the extracted text.
