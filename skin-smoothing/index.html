Skin Smoothing

Skin Smoothing has two parts. The first is a button labeled Quick Smooth. It finds every pixel in the image whose color falls inside a typical skin-tone range, and blends 55% of a 4-pixel blur into those pixels. This is a color test, not face detection, so it applies to the whole image: it smooths faces and hands, and it can also affect a wooden table, sand or a warm-colored wall that happens to fall in the same color range.

The second part is Heal Spot, for single blemishes. You drag a small box over a spot, press Heal Spot, and the tool replaces an elliptical area with a heavily blurred patch taken from the surrounding pixels. You can repeat this for each spot. Both methods are simple filters and not medical tools or diagnosis; they only change how a photo looks.

When to use Skin Smoothing

  • You want a light overall smoothing of skin tones in a portrait with a plain background.
  • A few isolated spots or small blemishes distract from an otherwise good photo.
  • You want to compare a quick global smoothing with a more targeted retouch.

Step by step

  1. Upload the portrait and open Skin Smoothing.
  2. For a global effect, press Quick Smooth and check the result, paying attention to any non-skin objects with similar colors.
  3. For blemishes, drag a small box on the preview image around one spot, then press Heal Spot.
  4. Repeat the spot removal for each blemish. The selection resets after every use.
  5. Zoom to 100% to check for blur around the patched areas, then download.

Tips for better results

  • If the background is warm-colored, expect Quick Smooth to soften it too. Use Face Retouch instead, where you paint only the skin.
  • Keep Heal Spot selections small, only slightly larger than the blemish. A large box leaves a visible blurred disk.
  • Check that freckles and natural skin texture are still visible. Heavy smoothing removes character.
  • Run Quick Smooth once. A second pass produces an obviously artificial look.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming Quick Smooth is limited to faces. It is a color-range filter and treats any skin-like color the same way.
  • Using Heal Spot on a spot near an edge, such as the eyebrow or lip line, where blur creates a smear.
  • Applying the effect to a photo with heavy makeup or warm lighting, where the color test selects almost everything.

What to expect

Heavy smoothing produces a plastic appearance. This is photo editing, not a diagnosis or treatment tool.

Questions about Skin Smoothing

Does Quick Smooth find faces?

No. It tests the color of each pixel against a typical skin-tone range, so it works on the whole image and can affect non-skin areas of a similar color.

How does Heal Spot work?

It takes the area around your box, blurs it by about 9 pixels, and places that blurred patch inside an ellipse over the spot. It is a blur patch, not a clone stamp.

Is this a medical or skin-treatment tool?

No. It is a photo-editing filter that changes pixels in an image. It does not analyze or diagnose skin.
