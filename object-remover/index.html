Object Remover

Object Remover fills the area you select using colors from the pixels around it. You choose the area with a Brush (size 8 to 90 on the preview scale, default 28, with an Eraser to take paint back off the mask) or with a Lasso, by clicking points around the object and closing the shape near the starting point. The algorithm then works inward from the boundary, repeatedly filling selected pixels with the average of neighboring pixels that are already known, and blends the result with the original using the edge of your selection.

That approach is deterministic and runs entirely on your device. It does not understand what was in the picture and it does not draw replacement content, so it produces smooth color fill rather than texture. It is effective for small marks and plain surfaces, and unsuitable for removing large objects from detailed scenes.

When to use Object Remover

  • You want to remove a dust speck, a sensor spot, a small blemish on a wall or a stray hair on a plain backdrop.
  • You need to cover small text such as a date stamp on your own photo, on a smooth surface.
  • You want to retouch a scan where the damage is narrow, like a scratch across a sky.

Step by step

  1. Upload the photo and open Object Remover.
  2. Choose Brush or Lasso. Paint or outline only the unwanted area and a thin margin around it. Use Eraser or Clear if you overshoot.
  3. Press Remove Selected Area and compare the result to the original at 100% zoom.
  4. If the fill looks smeared, press Undo, clear the selection and repeat with a smaller selection or several short passes.
  5. Download the result when the repaired area is not noticeable at the size you will display the image.

Tips for better results

  • Small, separate selections give better results than one large mask, because each pixel is filled from nearby neighbors.
  • Leave surrounding pixels unselected. If the whole image is selected the tool refuses, because it has nothing to copy from.
  • The selection is drawn on a preview roughly 340 pixels wide and scaled up to the full-resolution image, so on a large photo each stroke covers more real pixels than it appears to. Crop to the area you are repairing first if you need finer control.
  • Work on one mark at a time and check the result before moving on. Undo keeps up to 12 steps in the current session.
  • On textured surfaces such as grass or fabric, expect a smoother patch than the surroundings and consider adding a touch of noise afterward in another editor.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to remove a person or sign in front of a detailed background. The fill will look like a smooth blur.
  • Painting far outside the object. Extra selected area means more of the picture is replaced by averaged color.
  • Applying the tool repeatedly over the same area, which makes the smudge larger each time.

What to expect

This is deterministic boundary repair, not semantic AI reconstruction. It works best on small marks and simple backgrounds.

A small speck on a plain wall is suitable; removing a person in front of detailed lettering is not.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Models and libraries, where a tool needs them, download from public content networks. See the privacy policy for the full list.

Questions about Object Remover

Does Object Remover use generative AI?

No. It is a deterministic boundary-fill algorithm. That is why results are predictable and private, but it also means it cannot reconstruct complex content.

Why does the removed area look blurry?

The fill is built from averaged neighboring colors, which are smooth by nature. Use smaller selections and simple backgrounds for the best result.

Is there a limit on image size?

Inputs up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels are accepted. Large images take longer because the fill runs per pixel in your browser.
