Image Sharpener

Image Sharpener increases edge contrast with an unsharp mask. The tool makes a copy of your image blurred by 2 pixels, subtracts it from the original to find the edges, and adds that difference back, multiplied by a gain. The Sharpness slider runs from 0% to 100% and starts at 50%; internally the gain is the slider value times 1.6, so at 50% each edge difference is added at 0.8 times its size.

Sharpening makes existing edges look crisper. It cannot fix a photo that is out of focus or smeared by motion, and strong settings create bright and dark outlines (halos) along high-contrast edges. Use it as the final step of an editing sequence, after resizing.

When to use Image Sharpener

  • A photo is slightly soft after a resize or after noise reduction.
  • Text in a screenshot or scan looks a little fuzzy.
  • You want to bring out texture, such as fabric or foliage, in a photo that is in focus.

Step by step

  1. Resize your image to its final dimensions first, because sharpening before resizing is partly lost.
  2. Open Image Sharpener and begin with a low value such as 20 to 30%.
  3. Press Sharpen and zoom to 100%. Inspect high-contrast edges, such as a dark object against the sky, for light halos.
  4. Use Undo and lower the value if halos appear, or apply once more with a slightly higher value if the result is still soft.
  5. Download the sharpened image.

Tips for better results

  • View the result at the size people will see it. Sharpening that looks right at 100% can look harsh when the image is shown small.
  • Sharpening amplifies noise as well as detail. If the photo is grainy, reduce noise lightly first.
  • Two gentle passes usually look more natural than one strong pass, but check for halos after each.
  • Avoid sharpening skin heavily. It emphasizes pores and blemishes.

Common mistakes

  • Using a maximum setting to rescue a blurry photo. The result is halos and noise, not focus.
  • Sharpening before cropping or resizing, which changes the edge widths afterward.
  • Judging only the thumbnail. Halos are easy to miss on a small preview.

What to expect

Sharpening cannot correct severe motion blur or a badly missed focus point.

Text edges may become easier to read, but strong settings can create bright outlines.

Input, export and privacy

Questions about Image Sharpener

What does the percentage mean?

It scales how much of the detected edge difference is added back. At 100% the gain is 1.6 times the difference between the image and its 2-pixel blur.

Can I sharpen a blurry photo?

Only slightly. Sharpening improves apparent edge contrast but does not recover detail lost to motion or missed focus. See Remove Blur for a fixed-strength version, and the guide on sharpening workflows for realistic expectations.

Why do I see bright outlines after sharpening?

These halos appear when edge contrast is boosted too far. Undo and lower the strength.
