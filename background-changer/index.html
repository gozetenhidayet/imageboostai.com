Background Changer

Background Changer takes the subject cutout produced by the same detection step as Remove Background and places it over a new backdrop. The panel offers ten ready-made backdrops: White Studio, Light Gray, Dark Gray, Black, Sky Blue and Forest Green as flat colors, and Purple, Sunset, Ocean and Mint as diagonal two-color gradients. You can also load your own image with the Choose Image button. A preset applies as soon as you click it.

Open Background Changer

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

The tool does not generate a scene from a text prompt, and it does not adjust the lighting on your subject to match the new backdrop. Six theme buttons (Nature, Office, Studio, Wood, City and Abstract) open Unsplash in a new tab so you can download a free stock photo and then load it with Choose Image. Check the license of anything you download and keep the credit if the license asks for it.

When to use Background Changer

  • You need a plain white or light gray background for a catalog image, an ID-style portrait or a marketplace listing.
  • You want a brand-colored or gradient background behind a profile picture.
  • You are placing a person or object over a photograph and want a quick preview before refining the edges in a full editor.

Step by step

  1. Upload your photo and open Background Changer.
  2. Click one of the ten presets, or press Choose Image to use a photo from your device.
  3. Wait for the status line. The first run downloads the segmentation model, which can take a few seconds.
  4. Review the boundary at 100% zoom. Look for a halo of the old background color along the edge of the subject.
  5. Download as PNG for the best quality, or as JPEG if the file size matters more and the new background is fully opaque.

Tips for better results

  • Pick a backdrop with a similar color temperature to the original scene. A warm indoor photo on a cool blue gradient looks pasted even with a perfect cutout.
  • A mid-tone gradient such as Ocean or Purple tends to hide small cutout flaws better than pure white does, because the eye has less contrast to latch on to.
  • For a stock photo backdrop, choose an image with blurred or simple content so it does not compete with the subject.
  • Each preset replaces the previous background. If a result is not right, use Undo and try another preset rather than stacking changes.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring light direction. A subject lit from the left placed in front of a sunset on the right will look inconsistent.
  • Choosing a background whose colors match the subject's clothing or hair, which makes the outline vanish.
  • Using a low-resolution background image and expecting it to look sharp. The image is scaled to fill the frame, so pick one at least as large as your subject photo.

What to expect

Subject cutout quality limits the result. This does not generate a scene from a prompt.

For a catalog photo, choose a neutral white or pale gray background before adding a watermark.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Models and libraries, where a tool needs them, download from public content networks. See the privacy policy for the full list.

Questions about Background Changer

Can I type a prompt and get a new background?

No. Background Changer only composites your subject over one of the ten presets or an image you choose. It is not a text-to-image tool.

Which files can I use for the new background?

The Choose Image button accepts any image type your browser can open, such as JPG, PNG or WebP.

Why does my subject look cut out against the new scene?

The tool changes only the backdrop. If the colors, brightness and shadows of the subject do not match the new scene, adjust them afterward with Color Correction or Brightness & Contrast.
