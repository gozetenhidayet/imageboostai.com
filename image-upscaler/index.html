Image Upscaler

Image Upscaler makes a picture larger by resampling it at 2×, 4× or 8× of its current width and height. It uses the browser's high-quality smoothing when it creates the extra pixels, then applies a light sharpening pass (an unsharp mask at half strength) so the enlarged edges do not look soft. Clicking a scale button applies it immediately.

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

This is interpolation, not super-resolution. The new pixels are calculated from their neighbors, so the picture becomes bigger without gaining real detail. The output is capped at 6,000 pixels on the longest side. If the requested size would exceed that, the result is scaled down proportionally to fit. For example, a 1200 × 800 image at 2× becomes 2400 × 1600, at 4× becomes 4800 × 3200, and at 8× would be 9600 × 6400 so it is reduced to 6000 × 4000.

When to use Image Upscaler

  • You need a larger canvas so an image meets a minimum size requirement for a form, listing or upload.
  • You are preparing a small graphic for a larger layout and want smoother edges than a simple stretch would give.
  • You need to enlarge a screenshot or logo slightly for a presentation.

Step by step

  1. Upload the image and note its current width and height in the status line.
  2. Open Image Upscaler and choose 2×, 4× or 8×. Pick the smallest factor that meets your need.
  3. Wait for the result and read the toast message, which reports the final dimensions.
  4. Zoom to 100% and inspect edges and text for blur or halos.
  5. Download the PNG. Compress it afterwards with Image Compressor if the file is large.

Tips for better results

  • Choose 2× whenever 2× is enough. Each additional doubling makes soft edges and compression artifacts more obvious.
  • Start from the best original you have. A low-quality JPEG upscaled four times shows the blocks four times larger.
  • Output is PNG, which can be very large at high factors. Convert or compress for web use.
  • For print, check the pixel dimensions against the print size you need, as explained in the guide on pixels and DPI.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting new detail such as readable text or sharper faces to appear. Interpolation cannot add what was never captured.
  • Using 8× on a large image. The 6,000-pixel cap will shrink the result and the extra factor is wasted.
  • Upscaling repeatedly. Enlarging in steps does not give better quality than a single enlargement.

What to expect

Interpolation does not recover detail that was never captured. The output is limited to 40 megapixels.

A 1200 × 800 source at 2× becomes 2400 × 1600; it is larger, not a newly detailed photograph.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Questions about Image Upscaler

Is this the same as AI upscaling?

No. AI upscalers use trained models that predict plausible detail. This tool uses standard resampling plus mild sharpening, so it is faster and private, but it does not add detail.

Why is my 8× result smaller than expected?

The longest side is limited to 6,000 pixels. If the 8× size would be larger, the tool reduces it proportionally.

Will upscaling improve print quality?

It can help you reach a required pixel count, but the image will look as sharp as the original at its original size, only larger. Start from the highest-resolution source you can find.
