Colorize Old Photo

Colorize Old Photo applies a single tint across the whole picture. It uses the browser's color blend mode, which takes the hue and saturation from the tint you choose and keeps the brightness values of your photo. The result is a uniformly toned image, such as sepia, rather than a realistic recreation of the original colors.

Open Colorize Old Photo

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

Four presets are available (Sepia, Warm, Cool Blue and Vintage Rose), plus a color picker for any custom tint. An intensity slider from 10% to 100% controls how strongly the tint is applied, starting at 70%. Clicking a preset applies it immediately. The tool does not recognize skin, sky or clothing and it cannot recover historical colors, so it is best described as a toning effect.

When to use Colorize Old Photo

  • You want a warm sepia or cool blue tone on a black-and-white photograph.
  • You are creating a vintage look for an invitation, poster or social post.
  • You want to unify the color of several scans of different tones before placing them side by side.

Step by step

  1. Upload a black-and-white or desaturated photograph.
  2. Open Colorize Old Photo and set Intensity. Start lower than the default if you want a subtle toning.
  3. Click a preset, or choose a custom color with the picker and press Colorize.
  4. Compare with the original and use Undo to try another preset or intensity.
  5. Download the result, or refine contrast afterward with Brightness & Contrast.

Tips for better results

  • Because the photo's brightness is preserved, tint choice changes the mood but not the contrast. Adjust contrast separately.
  • Low intensity values around 30 to 50% produce a gentle tone that keeps the black-and-white character.
  • For a convincing sepia, pick a brown-orange tint of low saturation rather than a bright orange.
  • Apply the tint last. Contrast and sharpness adjustments done afterwards can shift the apparent color.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting realistic skin tones, blue skies or green grass. A single tint cannot assign different colors to different objects.
  • Using a very saturated custom color at 100%. It flattens the image into one monochrome hue.
  • Presenting a toned image as a restored or historically accurate color photograph.

What to expect

This is global tinting, not automatic object-aware colorization or historical color recovery.

A sepia tint gives a monochrome photo a warm tone without claiming the original clothing colors.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Questions about Colorize Old Photo

Will it color a black-and-white photo realistically?

No. It applies one tint to the entire image. Realistic colorization would need a model that recognizes objects, and this tool does not include one.

What is the difference between this and sepia filters elsewhere?

It is the same kind of effect, a color-blend tint, with the added ability to choose any custom color and intensity.

Can I tint only part of the picture?

Not in this tool, which affects the whole image. For selected areas, use Object Replace, which recolors the area you brush or outline.
