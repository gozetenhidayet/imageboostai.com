Image to Text OCR

Image to Text OCR reads printed text from a picture and gives it back as editable text. It runs Tesseract, an open-source recognition engine, in your browser through the Tesseract.js library. The first time you use it, the engine and the language data for the language you picked are downloaded, which can take a few seconds. After the text is recognized it appears in an editable box, with a Copy text button and a download link for a plain TXT file.

Open Image to Text OCR

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

Twelve languages are available: English, Turkish, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Arabic, Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Korean. Choose the language that matches the text in the photo. Recognition quality depends heavily on the input. Clean, high-contrast, printed text works well. Handwriting, decorative fonts, curved pages and low-resolution scans are often misread, so always check the result.

When to use Image to Text OCR

  • You need to copy text from a screenshot, a photographed page or a scanned document.
  • You want to search or edit text that exists only in an image.
  • You are digitizing a printed paragraph, a receipt or a sign for your own notes.

Step by step

  1. Upload a sharp image of the text. Crop it so that only the text area remains.
  2. Open Image to Text OCR and choose the language of the text.
  3. Press Extract Text. Watch the status line, which first reports loading the engine and then the reading progress.
  4. Read through the recognized text and correct mistakes in the editable box.
  5. Copy the text, or download it as a TXT file.

Tips for better results

  • Take the photo straight on, in even light, so that lines are horizontal and no shadow crosses the text.
  • Increase contrast with Brightness & Contrast, and enlarge small text with Image Upscaler before reading.
  • Crop away pictures, borders and columns you do not need. Complex page layouts confuse the recognition.
  • Verify names, numbers, dates and decimal points, which are the characters most often misread.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the wrong language, which produces random characters for accented letters or non-Latin scripts.
  • Trusting the result without proofreading, especially for numbers and legal or financial documents.
  • Uploading a very small or blurry screenshot and expecting accurate text.

What to expect

The first run downloads the engine and language data. Handwriting, complex layouts and low-resolution scans may not be recognized accurately.

Extract a printed paragraph, then verify names, dates, decimal points and accented characters before reusing it.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Models and libraries, where a tool needs them, download from public content networks. See the privacy policy for the full list.

Questions about Image to Text OCR

Is my image uploaded for OCR?

The recognition runs in your browser. The Tesseract.js library and the language data are downloaded from public servers on first use, but your image is processed on your device.

Can it read handwriting?

Not reliably. The engine is designed for printed text. Neat block handwriting may partly work, but cursive writing usually does not.

Why does it say no text was detected?

The image may be too small, too blurry, or low in contrast, or the wrong language may be selected. Try a sharper image, crop to the text, or choose another language.
