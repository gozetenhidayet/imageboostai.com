Noise Remover

Noise Remover reduces grain by blending a blurred copy of the image into the original. The blur radius grows with the Strength slider, from 1 pixel at 0% to 4.5 pixels at 100%, and the amount of blurred image mixed in grows up to 75% at full strength. The slider starts at 50%.

No account required. Keep your original and review the result before sharing.

This filter does not distinguish noise from detail and does not follow edges, so it softens everything a little, including hair, fabric and small text. It works best at low to moderate strength on photos with visible grain, such as night shots and photos taken at a high ISO setting.

When to use Noise Remover

  • A photo taken in low light has visible grain in the shadows or the sky.
  • A scan shows fine speckle on flat areas such as walls or backgrounds.
  • You want to smooth noise before upscaling or sharpening.

Step by step

  1. Upload the photo and zoom into a flat area, such as sky or a wall, to see the noise clearly.
  2. Open Noise Remover and set a moderate Strength, around 25 to 40% for light noise.
  3. Press Remove Noise and compare both flat areas and fine detail such as eyelashes and text.
  4. Undo and adjust the strength if detail looks too soft or noise is still visible.
  5. If the result looks slightly soft, finish with a gentle pass of Image Sharpener.

Tips for better results

  • Start with a lower strength than you think you need. The slider has a strong effect above about 60%.
  • Judge the result at 100% zoom, but also at the display size, since noise that is visible at 100% may be invisible when the picture is small.
  • Denoise before you sharpen. Reversing the order boosts the noise.
  • For portraits, use Face Retouch on selected skin instead of blurring the entire image.

Common mistakes

  • Using a high strength on an image full of detail. Textures such as hair and fabric turn smooth and waxy.
  • Applying the filter several times. The blur accumulates and detail is lost.
  • Expecting it to remove JPEG compression blocks. Those are structured artifacts, not random noise.

What to expect

Noise reduction can soften hair, fabric and small writing. It does not distinguish every detail from noise.

Use a low setting for a slightly grainy portrait and inspect eyebrows before exporting.

Input, export and privacy

The workspace accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP, GIF and HEIC images up to 25 MB and 40 megapixels; some devices need smaller files. Your image is processed in your browser and is not sent to our servers. Questions about Noise Remover

Does it keep edges sharp?

No. The filter is a uniform blur blend, so edges soften along with noise. Use a low strength and sharpen lightly afterward if needed.

What strength should I use?

For light grain, 20 to 40% is often enough. Use higher values only on noisy images that have little fine detail.

Can it remove color noise from night photos?

It will reduce the appearance of both brightness and color speckle, but it does so by smoothing everything. For heavy noise you may need a dedicated denoising program.
