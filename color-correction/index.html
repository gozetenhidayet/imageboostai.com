Color Correction

Color Correction gives you five sliders, each from −100 to +100, plus an Auto-Enhance button. Exposure brightens or darkens the whole image in photographic stops: +100 doubles the light and −100 halves it. Shadows and Highlights lift or lower only the darker or lighter parts of the picture by up to 70 levels, with the effect fading smoothly toward the middle tones. Temperature moves the balance toward warm (more red, less blue) or cool by up to 32 levels, and Tint moves it toward magenta or green by up to 16 levels.

The Auto-Enhance button does not analyze your photo. It simply sets the sliders to a fixed, gentle recipe (exposure +10, shadows +15, highlights −10, temperature +5, tint 0) and applies it. Everything here is an on-screen visual adjustment, not calibrated color management, and the tool does not read or write ICC color profiles.

When to use Color Correction

  • A photo taken indoors has an orange or blue cast that you want to neutralize.
  • Shadows are too dark or highlights are too bright and you want to rebalance them without changing the middle tones much.
  • Photos from different sources need to match each other before you place them in a collage or catalog.

Step by step

  1. Upload the photo and open Color Correction.
  2. Fix the overall cast first. Move Temperature toward the opposite of the cast, then use Tint if skin or white objects still look green or pink.
  3. Adjust Exposure for overall brightness, then Shadows and Highlights to recover detail in the dark and light areas.
  4. Press Apply and judge neutral objects, such as a white wall, and skin tones against the original.
  5. Use Undo to start again if the changes accumulate in the wrong direction, then download the result.

Tips for better results

  • Find something in the picture that should be neutral, like a white wall or gray pavement, and correct until it looks neutral.
  • Make small moves. A temperature change of 10 to 15 is already visible.
  • Raise Shadows slightly to reveal detail, but watch for gray, washed-out blacks.
  • Apply correction before sharpening and after cropping, so the tool measures the part of the picture you will keep.

Common mistakes

  • Correcting by eye on a screen with a strong color setting, such as night mode. View the result with filters off.
  • Pushing Exposure to fix a photo that is already clipped. Pure white and pure black areas have no detail to recover.
  • Using Auto-Enhance expecting an analysis-based result. It always applies the same fixed values.

Questions about Color Correction

What does Auto-Enhance do?

It sets Exposure to +10, Shadows to +15, Highlights to −10, Temperature to +5 and Tint to 0, and applies the correction. It does not examine your image, so treat it as a quick starting recipe.

How is Exposure measured?

The slider value divided by 100 is used as a power of two, so +100 multiplies brightness by 2 (one stop brighter) and −100 multiplies it by one half.

Does this replace professional color management?

No. It adjusts how the picture looks on screen and does not use or change embedded ICC profiles. For print workflows use a color-managed editor.
